# Scoring & calibration guide

Consistent scores are what make coaching trustworthy over time. Use this to calibrate.

## The 0–100 scale and bands

Default bands (a scorecard can override them in `scoring.bands`):

| Band       | Range  | Meaning                                            |
|------------|--------|----------------------------------------------------|
| great      | 80–100 | Textbook. Use as a teaching example.               |
| good       | 60–79  | Solid; minor gaps to tighten.                      |
| developing | 40–59  | Partially done; clear room to improve.             |
| poor       | 0–39   | Missing or counterproductive.                      |

## How to land a score for a criterion

1. Start at the criterion's `what_great_looks_like`. How many of those behaviors are
   present, executed well, and **evidenced**?
2. Check `what_poor_looks_like`. Any anti-patterns present pull the score down.
3. Weight by *quality*, not just presence. Asking the future-state question but not
   listening to the answer isn't a great score.
4. Require evidence. No quote → you cannot justify a high score; reflect that.

Rough anchors:
- **90+** — exemplary; you'd show this clip to the team.
- **75–85** — strong; one small thing away from exemplary.
- **60–70** — competent; the criterion was met but not pressed home.
- **45–55** — attempted but shallow or incomplete.
- **<40** — absent, or done in a way that hurt the call.

## Avoid these calibration traps
- **Grade inflation.** If everything is 80+, the report stops being useful. An average
  real call sits 50–65 overall.
- **Halo effect.** A great opener doesn't make the close great. Score each criterion
  on its own evidence.
- **Rewarding effort over outcome.** "Tried to book a next step" but didn't ≠ good.
- **Coaching the buyer.** You evaluate the rep. The buyer's behavior is context.

## Overall score
Weighted average of criterion scores, weights normalized to sum to 1. A single
catastrophic-but-low-weight miss shouldn't tank an otherwise strong call — but if a
high-weight criterion (e.g. quantified priority, next steps) is poor, the overall
should clearly reflect it.
