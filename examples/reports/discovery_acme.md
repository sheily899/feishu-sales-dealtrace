## What to improve

### 🎯 Close on a calendared next step, not a summary _(priority: high)_
After excellent discovery, the call ended with 'send me a summary' — an open loop with no date and no commitment. A document is not a next step; a meeting on the calendar is. This is the single biggest risk to the deal.
  > **Sam:** Sure, send that over. I'll take a look.

  **Try instead:** _I'll have that summary to you Thursday. Rather than you reading it cold, can we grab 30 minutes Friday so I can walk you and ideally your VP of Finance through it and the rough ROI? I'll send an invite for 2pm — does that work, or is morning better?_

### 🎯 Turn the impact into a number a CFO would accept _(priority: high)_
Jordan got hours (15/week) and a gut figure ('hundreds of thousands') but didn't co-build a defensible model. Before the Series B board, Sam will need a number he can defend. Anchor it together on the call so it's the buyer's number, not the vendor's.
  > **Sam:** Hard to quantify exactly but I'd say hundreds of thousands of dollars isn't crazy.

  **Try instead:** _Let's make that concrete. Fifteen hours a week is ~0.4 of an analyst — call it $X of loaded cost a year just in wrangling. Add the two or three resource decisions you'd have made differently — what would one of those have been worth? If we land around $Y, is that the number you'd be comfortable taking to the board?_

### 🎯 Secure the stakeholders, don't outsource the multi-thread _(priority: medium)_
Jordan correctly identified that Finance and Operations own sign-off, then left it to Sam to 'feel it out.' Single-threaded deals stall. Make it easy and low-risk for Sam to bring them in now, while the pain is hot.
  > **Sam:** Maybe. Let me feel it out with them first.

  **Try instead:** _Totally fair to socialize it first. To make that easy — want me to send a short, two-paragraph summary written for them, framed around the board-prep angle your Head of Ops has been pushing? Then if it resonates, the three of you join the Friday session. What's the best way to get on her radar?_

### 🎯 Map the decision process, not just the people _(priority: medium)_
Knowing who signs off is half of it. The steps, criteria, and budget remain unknown — the budget question got deflected and wasn't revisited from a different angle. You don't need an exact number, but you do need the shape of how a yes happens.
  > **Sam:** I honestly don't know yet. We haven't gotten that far. I'm in discovery mode myself.

  **Try instead:** _No problem on exact budget. Help me understand the path instead: if you and the team decided this was worth doing, what would the steps to a signed agreement look like — and is there budget allocated for this kind of project this year, or would it need to be carved out?_
